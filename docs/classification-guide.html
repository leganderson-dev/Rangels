Rangels V0.10 · visual classification guide

Every transaction answers three different questions.

Purpose, Context, and Bucket are deliberately separate. They describe what happened, where it belongs financially, and—only when relevant—which part of the Household plan it consumed.

This is an explanatory document only. It does not change the Rangels application.

The deliberately simple money plumbing

The split is parallel. Daily Expenses receives 60%, Splurge 10%, Smile 10%, and Fire Extinguisher 20% directly from the same Household income tank. Daily Expenses does not pour into Smile.
Businesses and properties keep their own results first. Only an amount deliberately made available to the Household enters this tank. Internal transfers still remain ledger-neutral.

Worked examples

Investment property

The property earns $1,000 rent but incurs $1,500 in costs.

Rental income +$1,000
Property expenses −$1,500
────────────────────────
Property result −$500

The result is a $500 property operating shortfall. A $500 transfer from a Household account can be shown as Property funding, but it stays ledger-neutral. This avoids counting the $500 twice: once as the real property expense and again as a transfer.

Why Household? Under the current simplified rule, only Household expenses may consume a Household Bucket. If it is classified as Property — Everton Park instead, the Bucket must be empty.

Why these rules exist

  1. Purpose and Context answer different questions. Electricity can be a Household cost, a Property cost, or a Business cost without inventing three unrelated purposes.
  2. Buckets remain a Household planning tool. Property and Business performance stay clear instead of being mixed into Daily Expenses or Splurge.
  3. Transfers remain ledger-neutral. Moving cash between your own accounts cannot create wealth or spending by itself.
  4. Actual costs are counted where they occur. The mortgage debit is the expense; the transfer that supplied cash for it is not another expense.
  5. The model can still explain cash movement. Transfer reason, Funding Context, source account, destination account, and +/− direction show where the money went.

How classification becomes planning and budgeting

These are four related layers, not four names for the same thing. Classification supplies the facts. The allocation plan divides eligible income. Budgets constrain particular kinds of spending. The balance ledger remembers what remains.

1 · CLASSIFY Describe actual transactions
  • Purpose: what?
  • Context: where?
  • Bucket: Household role?
Example: Groceries · Household · Daily Expenses
→
2 · PLAN Allocate eligible income
  • Daily Expenses 60%
  • Splurge 10%
  • Smile 10%
  • Fire Extinguisher 20%
This is the target split of incoming Household money.
→
3 · BUDGET Set spending limits
  • Groceries: $1,500
  • Restaurants: $400
  • Kids: $600
Budgets group one or more Purposes for a month.
→
4 · TRACK Carry balances forward
  • Opening entitlement
  • + new allocation
  • − actual use
  • ± audited movements
This is how Rangels should answer “how much do I have?”
Allocation plan: “Of this month’s eligible Household income, how much should each Bucket receive?” It is percentage-based.
Budget: “How much may we spend on these Purposes this month?” It is a dollar limit inside a Context and, for Household expenses, a Bucket. Budgets normally reset monthly; entitlement balances roll forward.

One numerical example

Assume $10,000 of eligible Household income is available this month. The Plan creates these target entitlements; classified expenses provide the actual use.

Allocated does not mean physically transferred. Rangels may show a $1,500 Fire Extinguisher entitlement even if the cash still sits in a general bank account. Physical accounts and planning balances are deliberately separate.

How much does each person have?

Account ownership tells us who made a purchase or contributed income. It does not, by itself, calculate personal entitlement. Splurge and Smile need an explicit member split plus a permanent roll-forward ledger.

Agreed behaviour: unused individual Splurge and Smile amounts roll forward. Only the owner of an accumulated amount may move it, the movement takes effect immediately, and it must be logged. Corrections should be reversing entries rather than silently rewriting history.

Who is charged? For a Splurge or Smile expense, the source account owner supplies the default member: Anderson account → Anderson allowance; Julia account → Julia allowance; Joint account → Shared. A manual override should remain available for exceptional cases.

The exact Fire Extinguisher destination split is still a product decision. Until it is defined, the shared balance can be calculated, but Rangels should not invent how much belongs to Mojo, debt, mortgages, or investments.

What Rangels can do today—and what is missing

Already present

  • Shared Household allocation plan stored in Supabase
  • 60 / 10 / 10 / 20 allocation targets
  • Monthly eligible income and target amounts on Plan
  • Monthly actual spending by classified Bucket
  • Monthly Budget groups made from selected Purposes
  • Account-owner contribution and purchase visibility

Still required for true balance tracking

  • Member split rules for Splurge and Smile
  • Opening balances for each member/Shared entitlement
  • Immutable allocation-ledger entries
  • Automatic monthly allocation credits
  • Roll-forward balance calculation across months
  • Audited entitlement transfers and reversals
  • Fire Extinguisher destination tracking
  • Budget scoping by Context and Household Bucket

Important: the current Plan page shows this month’s target minus this month’s classified use. It does not yet prove a permanent Anderson, Julia, or shared balance. That requires the ledger items listed above.

The shortest possible rule

Classify the economic event with Purpose + Context. Add a Bucket only when it is a Household expense. If it is a Transfer, record the reason and relevant Funding Context, then keep it out of income, expenses, tax, and Buckets.
