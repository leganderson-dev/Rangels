Every transaction answers three different questions.

Purpose, Context, and Bucket are deliberately separate. They describe what happened, where it belongs financially, and—only when relevant—which part of the Household plan it consumed.

This is an explanatory document only. It does not change the Rangels application.

The deliberately simple money plumbing

The split is parallel. Daily Expenses receives 60%, Splurge 10%, Smile 10%, and Fire Extinguisher 20% directly from the same Household income tank. Daily Expenses does not pour into Smile.
Businesses and properties keep their own results first. Only an amount deliberately made available to the Household enters this tank. Internal transfers still remain ledger-neutral.

Worked examples

Investment property

The property earns $1,000 rent but incurs $1,500 in costs.

Rental income +$1,000
Property expenses −$1,500
────────────────────────
Property result −$500

The result is a $500 property operating shortfall. A $500 transfer from a Household account can be shown as Property funding, but it stays ledger-neutral. This avoids counting the $500 twice: once as the real property expense and again as a transfer.

Why Household? Under the current simplified rule, only Household expenses may consume a Household Bucket. If it is classified as Property — Everton Park instead, the Bucket must be empty.

Why these rules exist

  1. Purpose and Context answer different questions. Electricity can be a Household cost, a Property cost, or a Business cost without inventing three unrelated purposes.
  2. Buckets remain a Household planning tool. Property and Business performance stay clear instead of being mixed into Daily Expenses or Splurge.
  3. Transfers remain ledger-neutral. Moving cash between your own accounts cannot create wealth or spending by itself.
  4. Actual costs are counted where they occur. The mortgage debit is the expense; the transfer that supplied cash for it is not another expense.
  5. The model can still explain cash movement. Transfer reason, Funding Context, source account, destination account, and +/− direction show where the money went.
The shortest possible rule

Classify the economic event with Purpose + Context. Add a Bucket only when it is a Household expense. If it is a Transfer, record the reason and relevant Funding Context, then keep it out of income, expenses, tax, and Buckets.
